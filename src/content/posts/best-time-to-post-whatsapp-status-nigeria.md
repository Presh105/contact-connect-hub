There is no single magic hour for WhatsApp Status. Your best time depends on who your customers are, whether they work in an office, run a shop or study, and when they have a spare minute to scroll. What we can do is give you a sensible starting schedule based on how most Nigerians use their phones, and a simple method to find out what works for your own audience.

> These time slots are a starting point based on common daily routines, not guaranteed results. Test them with your own audience before you commit.

## Why timing matters on Status

Status updates last 24 hours, but attention does not. Most people open the Status tab in short bursts, watch the first few updates in the list and leave. WhatsApp orders the list by recency and by who you have been talking to, so posting just before people open the app helps you appear near the top. Posting when your audience is busy or asleep means your update sits unseen.

## A starting schedule for Nigerian audiences

### Early morning, about 6:30 to 8:30am

People check their phones while getting ready and commuting, especially in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt where travel takes time. Good for: "Good morning" offers, daily specials, new arrivals, motivational or helpful tips. Keep posts short and clear because people are in a hurry.

### Lunch break, about 12:30 to 2:00pm

Office workers, students and traders take a break and scroll. This is a strong slot for product posts and discounts. Photos with price and a clear call to action work well.

### Early evening, about 5:30 to 7:30pm

People are heading home or relaxing after work. Good for: customer proof, delivery photos, longer captions and offers that need a little thought.

### Late evening, about 8:00 to 10:00pm

This is often the busiest Status time. People are at home, on data bundles or Wi-Fi and in a browsing mood. Good for: video, new product launches and "last chance" promotions.

Avoid posting after about 11pm unless your audience works nights, and avoid posting at the exact moment you know a power cut or network outage is common in your area.

## How many posts per day

For most small businesses, three to five updates a day works well. One in each major slot is easy to manage and does not overwhelm viewers. Posting ten or more updates at once makes people tap through quickly or mute you. If you have a lot to share, spread it across the day rather than dumping it together. ## Match content to the time of day

- **Morning:** greetings, quick tips, daily specials, "available today" lists.
- **Lunch:** product photos with prices, flash sales.
- **Evening:** customer reviews, behind-the-scenes, how-to content.
- **Late evening:** video, launches, stories that take a minute to watch.

## How to find your own best time: a one-week test

1. Pick four time slots from the list above.
2. For seven days, post one similar update in each slot. Keep the content type as consistent as you can.
3. After each post, open the viewer list at the end of the day and write down the number of views. Our guide on [checking who viewed your Status](/blog/how-to-check-who-viewed-whatsapp-status) shows where to find them.
4. Also note how many replies or orders came from each slot.
5. After seven days, keep the two best slots and swap the weakest two for new times. Repeat for another week.

After a month you will have real data about your own audience, which is worth more than any general advice.

## Special days and seasons

- **Weekends.** People sleep later and browse longer. Shift your morning slot to 9 or 10am.
- **Month-end and salary week.** Spending rises when salaries arrive. Increase product posts and offers.
- **Festive periods.** Around Christmas, Eid and school resumption, people shop more and scroll more. Post earlier and more specifically, such as "Order by Friday for Christmas delivery."
- **Rainy season and fuel disruptions.** If your audience commutes less or networks are slow, light photos beat heavy videos.

## Common timing mistakes

- Posting everything at once and then going silent for the rest of the day.
- Posting only at night because that is when you are free. Use a phone reminder or schedule time in your day.
- Ignoring your time zone. If you sell to Nigerians abroad, check their local time, because 8pm in Lagos is mid-afternoon in some places.
- Changing too many things at once, so you cannot tell what worked.

## Worked example: a small bakery testing its slots

Imagine a home baker in Ibadan who sells cakes and small chops. She starts with the four slots above and notes the results in a notebook. Her morning posts, a simple "orders open for today" message, get steady views but few replies. Her lunch posts with a cake photo and price get more replies from office workers. Her late evening video of a cake being decorated gets the most views, but replies come mostly the next morning.

From this she learns three things. Mornings are good for reminders, lunch is good for direct orders and late evening is good for attention. So she keeps her four slots but changes what goes into each one, rather than posting the same thing at every time. The lesson is that the best time depends on the job each post has to do, not just the clock.

## Signs your timing is working

Good timing shows up in more than one number. Look for views arriving within the first hour after you post, replies that mention seeing your Status that day and fewer people asking questions you already answered in the post. If views come in late at night or the next day, the slot is probably too early or too busy for your audience. Move it by an hour and test again for a few days before judging.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the time zone of my phone matter?

WhatsApp shows when you posted using each viewer's local time, so your audience in Nigeria sees your update at Nigerian times. The only time you need to think about it is when you sell to people abroad.

### Is it better to post once in the morning or spread posts out?

Spreading posts out is usually better. Each new update gives people another reason to open your Status, and viewers who missed the morning post can still catch the evening one.

### What if my customers are night-shift workers or students?

Then your best times will be different from the general schedule. Run the one-week test using late-night and mid-afternoon slots, and trust your own numbers over general advice.

### Should I delete old updates before posting new ones?

There is no need. Updates disappear after 24 hours. Delete one only if it has an error, such as a wrong price, and replace it with the corrected version. Wrong prices cause disputes, as our guide on [pricing products on WhatsApp](/blog/how-to-price-products-on-whatsapp-nigeria) explains.

## Build a posting habit you can keep

The best schedule is one you will actually follow. Set two or three phone alarms labelled with the post type, such as "lunch product post," and keep a folder of ready photos so you are never scrambling. If you have a bad week, do not try to make up for it by posting everything at once. Return to your normal slots the next day.

## Final advice

Start with the four slots above, test for a week, then adjust. Pair good timing with a relevant contact list and useful content, and your views will rise. Without those, even perfect timing will not rescue a Status that the right people have not saved.
