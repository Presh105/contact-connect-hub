Running out of things to post is the number one reason small businesses fall silent on WhatsApp Status. The second reason is that every post looks the same. If your Status is only "Available. DM for price," people stop watching. This guide gives you 30 ideas to rotate through, five caption formulas you can copy and a simple way to plan a full week in about thirty minutes.

## Start with a content mix

Across a week, aim for roughly this balance: 40 percent product and offers, 25 percent proof, 20 percent helpful content and 15 percent personality. It keeps your Status interesting and builds trust, which is what turns viewers into buyers. For timing, see our [posting schedule for Nigeria](/blog/best-time-to-post-whatsapp-status-nigeria).

## Product and offer ideas

1. **Hero product of the day.** One clear photo, price, size or colour options and how to order.
2. **New arrival.** "Just landed" with the first available stock count.
3. **Limited stock alert.** "Only 6 left" works only if it is true.
4. **Bundle deal.** Two or three items sold together at a small saving.
5. **Weekend special.** A short-lived offer with a deadline.
6. **Price list screenshot.** Clean, readable and updated. Keep it short.
7. **Restock notice.** Tell people the item they asked about is back.
8. **Delivery information.** Areas you cover, usual delivery time and cost.

## Proof ideas

9. **Customer screenshot.** A thank-you message, with permission and the number hidden.
10. **Delivery photo.** The parcel in the customer's hands.
11. **Before and after.** Works for hair, cleaning, repairs, makeup and design.
12. **Order count.** "42 orders packed this week."
13. **Unboxing video.** A customer or you opening the package.
14. **Five-star review.** From Google, Instagram or your chats.
15. **Repeat customer story.** A short sentence about why someone keeps ordering.

## Helpful content ideas

16. **How to use it.** A 15-second tip about your product.
17. **Common mistake.** "Three mistakes people make when buying a wig."
18. **Care instructions.** How to keep the product lasting.
19. **Buyer's checklist.** What to check before paying for this kind of item.
20. **Myth buster.** Correct one common belief in your market.
21. **Quick comparison.** Option A versus Option B and who each suits.
22. **Question of the day.** Ask something your customers care about and reply to answers.

## Personality ideas

23. **Behind the scenes.** Packing orders, sourcing at the market, setting up the shop.
24. **Meet the team.** One short post about who helps you.
25. **Your story.** Why you started and what you stand for.
26. **A small win.** A milestone, such as your first hundred customers.
27. **Day in the life.** Three quick clips from your day.
28. **Local pride.** A shout-out to your area, a local event or a fellow vendor.
29. **Thank-you post.** Appreciate customers by name with permission.
30. **Honest update.** Delays, stock issues or price changes explained plainly. Honesty builds more trust than silence.

## Five caption formulas that get replies

### 1. Problem, solution, action

"Tired of phone chargers that die in a month? Our 20W fast charger comes with a six-month warranty. Reply CHARGER for the price."

### 2. Offer, deadline, action

"Ankara sets, N18,000 until Sunday. Sizes 8 to 18. Send your size to order."

### 3. Proof, offer, action

"Adaeze got her order in 2 hours in Lekki. Want yours today? Reply with your area."

### 4. Question, answer, action

"Which colour should we restock first, black or green? Reply with your pick and get 5 percent off."

### 5. Simple and direct

"New stock: men's sandals, sizes 40 to 45. N9,500. Delivery nationwide."

## Caption rules that work

- **One call to action only.** "Reply YES" or "Tap the link," not both.
- **Put the price in.** Hiding the price makes people feel they will be pitched.
- **Be specific.** "Fits iPhone 13 and 14" beats "Phone case available."
- **Keep it short.** Two or three lines for most posts.
- **Use plain language.** Write the way you would explain it to a friend.
- **Avoid fake urgency.** If "only 3 left" is not true, customers will notice and trust you less.

## Plan a week in thirty minutes

1. List your top three products and three common customer questions.
2. Fill a seven-day grid with four slots per day. Put a mix of ideas from above in each slot.
3. Batch-shoot photos and short videos in one session with good light. Our guide to [phone product photos and short videos](/blog/product-photos-with-your-phone-for-whatsapp-status) shows how.
4. Write captions in your notes app and copy them as needed.
5. At the end of the week, check which posts earned replies and repeat them. See [how to check who viewed your Status](/blog/how-to-check-who-viewed-whatsapp-status).

## Content ideas by type of business

The thirty ideas above work for almost any seller, but a few adjustments make them feel more natural for your trade.

- **Food and snacks.** Show the cooking process, the packaging and the delivery. Post the day's menu early, and a "last orders" reminder in the afternoon.
- **Fashion and tailoring.** Show fabric choices, measurements being taken and finished outfits on real people, with permission.
- **Beauty and hair.** Before and after photos do the heavy lifting. Add a short note on how long the style lasts and how to maintain it.
- **Phones and electronics.** Show the item working, list what is in the box and be clear about warranty terms and condition. Honest descriptions stop disputes. See [writing product descriptions that sell](/blog/write-product-descriptions-that-sell).
- **Services and freelancers.** Share a finished project, a client result or a quick tip from your field. Our guide for [freelancers finding clients on WhatsApp](/blog/freelancers-find-clients-on-whatsapp-nigeria) goes deeper.

## Turn one product into a full week of posts

Suppose you sell body creams. Monday, post the hero photo with price and sizes. Tuesday, show how you pack an order. Wednesday, share a customer message with permission. Thursday, give a quick tip on caring for dry skin. Friday, run a weekend special with a deadline. Saturday, show a day in your shop. Sunday, post an honest update about what is in stock for the week. Each post uses a different idea from the list, yet all of them point back to the same product. This approach keeps your Status varied without forcing you to invent new stock every day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many of these ideas should I use in one day?

Two to four is plenty. Pick one product post, one proof or tip post and, if you can, one personality post. You do not need to use all thirty in a month, only rotate them so nothing repeats too often.

### What if I have only one product?

Show it in different situations: being packed, being used, being delivered and being reviewed. One product can fill a whole week when you change the angle and the story.

### Can I post other people's content?

Only with their permission. Using a competitor's photos or a stranger's video as your own can hurt your reputation and may cause copyright complaints.

### How do I know a post worked?

Look at replies and orders, not only views. A post with fewer views that brings two orders beats one with many views and no messages.

## Keep it honest and compliant

Do not make claims you cannot support, and do not use other people's photos or customer messages without permission.
