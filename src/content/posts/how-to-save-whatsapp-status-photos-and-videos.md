A WhatsApp Status disappears after 24 hours, so it is normal to want to keep a good one. Maybe a friend posted a lovely wedding clip, a vendor shared a price list you need, or you posted a promo of your own and forgot to save the original. This guide explains the safe ways to save Status photos and videos on Android and iPhone, what to avoid, and why asking permission matters more than any trick.

## Why you cannot always just tap "download"

Status is built to be temporary. WhatsApp shows it to the people who have the poster's number saved (and who the poster allows through their privacy settings), then removes it after a day. Because of that, you will not always find a plain save button on someone else's Status, and the options change as WhatsApp updates the app. The wording and menus also vary by phone model and app version, so use the steps below as a guide rather than an exact map.

The most important point comes first: a Status belongs to the person who posted it. Saving it for your own reference is one thing. Reposting it, selling with it or sharing it publicly without permission is another.

## Saving your own Status photos and videos

This is the easy and fully legitimate case. If you posted it, you are free to keep it.

1. Open the Status tab in WhatsApp and find **My status**.
2. Open your own update and look for the menu or the options that appear when you view it. Many versions let you save or download the item to your phone.
3. If you do not see that option, share it to yourself or to a chat and save it from there, or use the screenshot or screen recording methods below.

A better habit is to save the original before you post. If you took a product photo with your camera, it is already in your gallery. If you edited it inside WhatsApp, check your photo gallery or the app's settings for an option to keep edited media. Our guide to [taking good product photos with your phone](/blog/product-photos-with-your-phone-for-whatsapp-status) also shows how to keep a clean folder of originals so you never need to rescue a Status afterwards.

## Saving someone else's Status the built-in way

If a contact has posted something you want to keep, the safest approach is to use what your phone already provides, and to ask first.

### Ask the person

A short message is enough: "Your Status of the Lagos event was lovely. Can you send me the photo, please?" Many people are happy to send the original, and the original has better quality than a copy of a copy. It also keeps the relationship friendly, which matters if the person is a customer, supplier or business partner.

### Screenshot (photos)

On both Android and iPhone you can take a screenshot while the Status is open. The button combination depends on the phone, so check your phone's help pages if you are not sure. Android users can find general guidance on the [Google support site](https://support.google.com/), and iPhone users on the [Apple support site](https://support.apple.com/). A screenshot captures what is on the screen, so you may need to crop out the name bar and buttons afterwards.

### Screen recording (videos)

Both Android and iPhone offer a screen recording feature in most recent versions. You start recording, play the Status, then stop. The result is a video file in your gallery. Quality is lower than the original, and the recording may capture notifications that pop up, so switch on Do Not Disturb first.

### Forwarding or replying

Some updates can be replied to. A reply creates a chat in which the person may send the file back to you. This also notifies them, which is usually good: it shows you are asking rather than quietly taking.

## What are Status Saver apps?

You will see many apps in app stores and on social media called "Status Saver", "Story Saver" or "Status Downloader". They promise to show you contacts' Status updates in a list and let you save them with one tap. Some work by reading the temporary files that WhatsApp keeps on the phone for the updates you have already viewed.

Because they are not part of WhatsApp, they are not controlled or checked by WhatsApp. Whether a particular app works, stays available or is safe can change at any time. Here is why we advise caution.

### The risks

- **Malware and adware.** Free apps that exist mainly to show adverts can load aggressive ads, track your activity or hide harmful code. Apps installed from random links or APK files outside the official store are the riskiest.
- **Over-wide permissions.** A status saver needs access to your files. If it also asks for contacts, SMS, call logs or accessibility control that has nothing to do with saving a photo, treat that as a red flag.
- **Account takeover.** Some fake apps and websites ask you to enter your phone number and a verification code, or tell you to scan a QR code to "connect WhatsApp". A verification code should never be given to anyone. A QR scan can link your account to someone else's device. Read more about this kind of trick in our guide to [spotting WhatsApp scams in Nigeria](/blog/spot-whatsapp-scams-nigeria).
- **Fake "modded" WhatsApp.** Some sites advertise a modified WhatsApp with built-in Status downloading. These unofficial versions can steal data, and using them can lead WhatsApp to restrict your number.
- **Privacy and fairness.** The person who posted did not agree to a copy being stored on your phone, and apps that save in bulk make it easy to keep things people never meant you to keep.

### If you still choose to use one

We do not recommend relying on these apps, but if you decide to try one for saving content you have a right to use, follow these rules:

1. Install only from the official store for your phone, and read the developer name and recent reviews.
2. Refuse any request for your WhatsApp verification code, your two-step verification PIN or a QR scan.
3. Deny permissions that are not needed for reading files.
4. Do not use it on the phone that holds your business number and customer chats if you can avoid it.
5. Delete it if it behaves strangely, shows full-screen adverts or asks for new permissions after an update.

On iPhone, apps of this type are much more limited, which is partly why screenshots and screen recording are the usual routes there.

## Permission, copyright and respect

Many Nigerians run small businesses on WhatsApp, and Status is where they show their work. A tailor in Aba posting a new design, a baker in Port Harcourt sharing a cake photo or a photographer in Lagos posting event pictures has done real work. Copying it and presenting it as your own is unfair, and it can also be a legal problem.

Some simple rules:

- **Keep for private reference only when it is harmless**, for example saving a friend's address card or a price list so you can find it later.
- **Ask before reposting.** If you want to share someone's content, get a clear yes, and credit them.
- **Never use another vendor's product photos to sell as if the goods were yours.** Customers who receive something different will complain, and your reputation suffers. Our guide on [building customer trust online](/blog/build-customer-trust-online-nigeria) explains how honesty drives repeat sales.
- **Be extra careful with photos of people.** Pictures of faces, children, IDs, receipts or medical documents can expose private information. Under Nigerian data protection rules, personal information has to be handled responsibly. You can read the plain-language overview in our article on the [Nigeria Data Protection Act and WhatsApp contacts](/blog/nigeria-data-protection-act-whatsapp-contacts) and the official site of the [Nigeria Data Protection Commission](https://ndpc.gov.ng/).
- **Do not use saved content to embarrass, threaten or blackmail anyone.** That is harmful and can lead to serious consequences.

Laws and platform policies change, so check the current rules if you plan to reuse content commercially.

## Protecting your own Status from being copied

If people can save your work this way, you may want to protect it.

- **Add a small watermark or your business name** in a corner of product photos. Do not make it so large that it ruins the picture.
- **Use Status privacy settings.** WhatsApp lets you choose who sees your updates, for example only selected contacts. The menu path varies, so look under the privacy section of settings.
- **Post lower detail for premium work.** Share a preview, and send full-quality files to paying customers directly.
- **Keep a record of the originals.** If someone steals your photo, the original file with its date helps prove it was yours.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Installing the first "Status Saver" that appears in a search result.
- Entering a verification code on a website that promises Status downloads.
- Posting someone else's screenshot as your own advert.
- Forgetting that screen recordings can capture private notifications.
- Saving everything to your phone until storage is full and the phone slows down. Clear old files regularly.
- Assuming people will not notice. Contacts often recognise their own photos.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does WhatsApp tell someone when I save their Status?

Not by itself. A screenshot or screen recording is not announced by WhatsApp to the poster. But the owner will know if you reply, and good manners say you should ask first.

### Can I save a Status after it has expired?

Usually not. Once the 24 hours have passed, the update is gone from WhatsApp. This is why saving your own work early, or asking the owner for the original, is the better plan.

### Is it illegal to save someone's Status?

Keeping a private copy for personal reference is generally treated differently from copying and publishing it. Reposting, selling or claiming someone's photo or video as yours may breach their rights. Rules differ and change, so when in doubt, ask permission.

### Are Status Saver apps from the Play Store always safe?

No. An official store lowers the risk but does not remove it. Check the developer, reviews and permissions, and never share a verification code or scan a QR code for any app.

## Final thoughts

The safest way to save a Status is simple: keep your own originals, ask others to send theirs, and use your phone's screenshot or screen recording tools for harmless personal reference.
