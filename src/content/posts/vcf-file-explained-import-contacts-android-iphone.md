A VCF file is the simplest way to move many contacts between phones at once. Businesses use VCF files to share customer lists, communities use them for contact exchange and individuals use them to back up their phone books. This guide explains what a VCF file is, how to import one on Android and iPhone, how to back up and export contacts and how many contacts your phone can handle.

## What is a VCF file?

VCF stands for **Virtual Contact File**, also called a **vCard**. It is a plain-text format that stores contact details such as name, phone number, email and address. One file can hold a single contact or thousands. Almost every phone, email service and contacts app can read it, which is why it is the standard way to share contacts.

A basic entry looks like this: a name, a phone number and a few formatting lines. You can open a VCF file in a text editor to see this, but you normally just import it into your phone.

## Why people use VCF files

- **Moving to a new phone** without relying on a SIM card.
- **Backing up** your contacts to a file or cloud account.
- **Sharing lists** with a team or community. Many WhatsApp business communities, including [Status Connect](/auth), distribute verified member contacts as VCF files.
- **Cleaning and editing** contacts in a spreadsheet before re-importing.

## How to import a VCF file on Android

### Method 1: Using the Contacts app

1. Download the VCF file to your phone. It will usually appear in Downloads.
2. Open the **Contacts** app.
3. Tap the menu (three lines or three dots), then **Settings**, then **Import** (the wording varies by brand).
4. Choose **VCF file** or **.vcf file** and select the file.
5. Choose where to save the contacts: your Google account or the phone. Google is best because it syncs and backs up.
6. Wait for the import to finish. Large files can take a few minutes.

### Method 2: Tap the file

Open your file manager, tap the VCF file and choose Contacts when asked. This works on many phones.

### Method 3: Google Contacts on a browser

On any browser, open [Google Contacts](https://contacts.google.com/), choose **Import**, upload the file and select it. Contacts will sync to your Android phone. This is helpful for big files.

## How to import a VCF file on iPhone

1. Open the file from **Files**, **Mail**, **Safari** or a chat app.
2. Tap the file. The iPhone will show a preview of the contacts.
3. Tap **Add All Contacts** (or **Add Contact** for a single one).
4. Choose whether to save into iCloud or On My iPhone.

Large VCF files can take a few minutes and the phone may seem slow while it processes them. Keep the screen on and stay on Wi-Fi if iCloud sync is on. Apple's own [contacts support pages](https://support.apple.com/) cover the latest steps for your iOS version.

## How to export or back up contacts

### Android

Open Contacts, go to Settings, tap **Export**, choose your account or all contacts and select **Export to .vcf file**. Save it to your phone and then copy it to Google Drive or email it to yourself. Better still, make sure **Sync** is turned on for your Google account so your contacts back up automatically.

### iPhone

Contacts are normally backed up to iCloud when it is turned on in Settings, then your name, iCloud, Contacts. To export a VCF file, sign in to iCloud.com in a browser, open Contacts, select all and choose **Export vCard**.

### A simple backup routine

- Turn on contact sync for Google or iCloud.
- Export a VCF file once a month and store it in a cloud folder.
- Before importing any large file, export your current contacts first so you can roll back.

## How many contacts can you save?

Modern phones can store thousands of contacts. The real limit is usually the account that holds them. Google accounts allow a very large number of contacts, but sync can slow down with huge lists. SIM cards store far fewer, often only a few hundred, so avoid saving to SIM. If you manage large lists, keep them in Google Contacts or iCloud and import in batches of a few thousand at a time.

## Tips for businesses and communities

- **Use clear names.** For example, "Ada - Lagos - Hair vendor" is easier to find than "Ada 2."
- **Add the country code.** Numbers saved as +234 work better with WhatsApp than numbers starting with 0.
- **Label imported groups.** Create a label or group such as "Status Connect 2026" so you can find or delete the batch later.
- **Respect privacy.** Only share and store contacts with permission. See our guide on [data protection and WhatsApp contacts](/blog/nigeria-data-protection-act-whatsapp-contacts).

## Preparing a clean contact list before you import

Most import headaches start with a messy list. Spending ten minutes on preparation saves a lot of trouble later.

- **Open the list in a spreadsheet first** if you have it as a CSV. Check that names are in one column and numbers in another, with no blank rows.
- **Remove obvious duplicates** so the same person is not saved three times.
- **Standardise numbers.** Pick one format, such as +234 followed by the number without the leading zero, and use it for every row.
- **Keep names short but meaningful.** A name plus a trade or city helps you recognise people months later.
- **Test with a small batch.** Import ten contacts, confirm they look right and only then bring in the full file.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I open a VCF file without importing it?

Yes. On a computer you can open it in any text editor to read the names and numbers. On a phone, tapping the file usually shows a preview, and you can choose not to add anything.

### Will importing a VCF file overwrite my existing contacts?

No. Importing adds new entries. It does not replace what you already have, which is exactly why duplicates appear when the same person is in both lists. Back up first and merge duplicates afterwards.

### Can I share one contact as a VCF instead of the whole phone book?

Yes. Open the contact, tap Share and choose a chat or email. The phone creates a small VCF for that one person. This is handy when a customer asks for a supplier's number.

### Is it safe to import a VCF file I received on WhatsApp?

The file itself only holds contact details, but the sender matters. Import lists only from people or communities you know, and check the names look sensible before saving. If a stranger sends a list and asks for anything in return, such as a code or a payment, walk away. Download VCF files only from sources you trust. A VCF file cannot contain a virus by itself, but links or instructions that come with it might lead to scams.
